## Key figures

- Headquartered at 2409 Merced St., Fresno, CA. Originated in 2005 as the Central California Regional Obesity Prevention Program, renamed Cultiva La Salud in 2012, became an independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit in 2020.
- Operates across **eight Central Valley counties: Fresno, Kern, Kings, Madera, Merced, San Joaquin, Stanislaus, and Tulare** — Fresno County is explicitly one of them.
- Program: **"converting existing food vendors in the city's food deserts into healthy food vendors for the Latino community — including outfitting vendors with new bicycle carts."** This program won the state's **"Let's Get Healthy California Innovation Challenge."**
- Program: **"provides medically tailored food boxes to elder Latinas, age 65 and older living in Fresno County."**
- Also runs farmers markets, community gardens, and school farm stands "in underserved neighborhoods," and a "HOPE Pathways Community HUB" where community health workers help residents navigate language, transportation, and access-to-care barriers.

## Summary

Cultiva La Salud is a Fresno-headquartered nonprofit that works on the *environment* side of food access — permanently converting existing corner-store and street-vendor infrastructure in Fresno's food deserts, rather than only distributing food — plus a specific program of medically tailored food boxes for isolated Fresno County seniors. Its model complements, rather than duplicates, a direct-distribution food bank, but none of the sources found quantify how many people or vendors it currently reaches.
