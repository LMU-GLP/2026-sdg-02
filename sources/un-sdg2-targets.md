# SDG Goal 2: Zero Hunger — Targets and Indicators

- **Source:** United Nations, Sustainable Development Goals knowledge platform
- **URL:** https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal2
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- Goal 2 aims to "End hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition and promote sustainable agriculture."
- **Target 2.1** (the one most relevant to this challenge): "By 2030, end hunger and ensure access by all people, in particular the poor and people in vulnerable situations, including infants, to safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year round." Measured by two indicators: prevalence of undernourishment (2.1.1), and prevalence of moderate or severe food insecurity based on the Food Insecurity Experience Scale, or FIES (2.1.2).
- **Global 2024 undernourishment: 8.2% of the global population**, down from 8.7% in 2022.
- **"around 2.3 billion people were moderately or severely food insecure" in 2024.**
- Global childhood stunting fell from 26.4% (2012) to 23.2% (2024).
- 30.7% of women of reproductive age were affected by anaemia in 2023.
- Target 2.2 also names a specific vulnerable group relevant to our Korea source: it calls for addressing "the nutritional needs of ... older persons," alongside infants, adolescent girls, and pregnant/lactating women.

## Direct quotes

> "By 2030, end hunger and ensure access by all people, in particular the poor and people in vulnerable situations, including infants, to safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year round."

> "around 2.3 billion people were moderately or severely food insecure" [2024]

## ⚠️ Do not compare the global 8.2%/2.3 billion figures directly to the LA or Seoul numbers

The global figures use FAO's undernourishment and FIES methodology at a national/global population level. The LA brief uses the USDA Household Food Security Module; the Seoul study uses a single-item variety-based question. All three are legitimate but not measuring identically, and none should be averaged or restated as equivalent.

## Summary

The UN's own target for this challenge is Target 2.1: ending hunger and ensuring year-round access to safe, nutritious, sufficient food, with explicit attention to vulnerable groups including older persons. Globally, food insecurity remains large in absolute terms (2.3 billion moderately or severely food insecure in 2024) even as the undernourishment rate has ticked down since 2022.
