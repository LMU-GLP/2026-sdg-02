# Food & Nutrition Insecurity in Los Angeles County

- **Source:** USC Dornsife Public Exchange research brief (data collected October 2025, published December 2025)
- **URL:** https://public-exchange.org/usc/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/12/Food-Nutrition-Insecurity-in-LA-County-October-2025.pdf
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- **1 in 4 (24%) of all L.A. County households experienced food insecurity in the past year.** Statistically similar to the 25% recorded in the 2024 report.
- Five-year trend: 17% (Dec 2021, ~553,000 households) → 24% (Jul 2022, ~802,000) → 30% (Jul 2023, ~986,000) → 25% (Oct 2024, ~852,000) → **24% (Oct 2025, ~814,000)**
- Among low-income households (<300% of the federal poverty line): **1 in 3 (35%)**, down from 41% in 2024 and 44% in July 2023.
- Nutrition insecurity dropped from 29% in October 2024 to **21%** in October 2025.
- **25% of food-insecure residents have incomes above 300% FPL.** The brief notes this group "has grown over time."
- 40% of all food-insecure households have at least one child.
- 60% of those with food insecurity are female; 57% are Hispanic/Latino; 53% are aged 18–40.

## Direct quotes

> "About 1 in 4 (24%) households experienced food insecurity in the past year."

> "Although food insecurity declined among low-income households from 41% in 2024 to 35% in 2025, the rate among all county households remained essentially unchanged (25% in 2024 and 24% in 2025). This indicates that food insecurity has not improved for higher-income residents."

> "the cost of living in L.A. is about 50% more than the national average"

## Context the brief gives

2025 was volatile in L.A.: January wildfires burned 57,500 acres and destroyed
more than 18,000 structures, and immigration enforcement operations meant "fear
of deportation has kept many families from public spaces, making it harder to
buy groceries or access food assistance."

## Method

USC's Understanding America Study, an internet panel run by the Center for
Economic and Social Research. Food insecurity measured with the USDA Short Form
Household Food Security Module.

## Summary

One in four L.A. County households is food insecure, a rate that has barely
moved since 2022 even as conditions improved for the poorest households. The
striking shift is who is affected: a quarter of food-insecure Angelenos now earn
above 300% of the federal poverty line, which means they earn too much to
qualify for SNAP but not enough to cover food in a city where living costs run
50% above the national average.
