# Food insecurity and its associated characteristics of the elderly in Seoul

- **Source:** Hyunjeong Park et al., *Nutrition Research and Practice*, 2025;19(1):117. Analysis of the Seoul Food Survey 2023.
- **URL:** https://e-nrp.org/DOIx.php?id=10.4162%2Fnrp.2025.19.1.117
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- 513 Seoul residents aged 65+ analyzed.
- **56.9% fell into the food-insecure group.** Breakdown of the single-item question: 43.1% "enough and various kinds of food," 37.3% "enough but not always various kinds," 16.3% "sometimes not enough to eat," 3.3% "often not enough to eat."
- Reasons, by category: **environmental 38.0%, physical 37.5%, financial 24.5%.**
- Most common single reason: "Insufficient money to purchase food" (24.5%), then "Insufficient time to purchase or prepare food" (21.6%), then **"Desired types of food are not available nearby" (14.9%).**
- **Elderly living alone were about four times more likely to be food insecure** (odds ratio 3.59, 95% CI 1.52–8.51).
- Two things were *protective*: higher **digital food literacy** (OR 0.63) and higher **food environment satisfaction** (OR 0.38).
- Korea's population aged 65+ passed nine million in 2024, 19.2% of the population, expected to pass 20% in 2025 — a "super-aged society."

## Direct quotes

> "The proportion of the food-insecure group was 56.9% among the total elderly in Seoul."

> "Educational programs designed to enhance the ability to purchase online food, use self-service kiosks in restaurants, and use food delivery apps would effectively decrease food insecurity among the elderly in Seoul."

## ⚠️ Do not compare this 56.9% to L.A.'s 24%

They measure different things. This study counts anyone who lacks *variety* as
food insecure ("enough but not always various kinds of food" = 37.3 of the 56.9
points). The USC L.A. brief uses the USDA module, which is about not having
enough food at all. Comparing the two headline numbers would be wrong. If this
team wants a like-for-like comparison, the closer figure is the 19.6% here who
"sometimes" or "often" did not have enough to eat — and even that isn't exact.

## Summary

Food insecurity among Seoul's elderly is driven more by environment and physical
ability than by money: not being able to get to the food, prepare it, or find
what's wanted nearby outweighs not affording it. The study's own recommendation
is a product idea with evidence behind it — teach older adults to use delivery
apps, kiosks, and online grocery ordering, because digital food literacy
measurably lowers the risk.
