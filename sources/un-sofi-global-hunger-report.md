# The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2025 (SOFI)

- **Source:** FAO, IFAD, UNICEF, WFP and WHO — joint annual UN report; FAO newsroom summary
- **URL:** https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/global-hunger-declines--but-rises-in-africa-and-western-asia--un-report/en
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- **"8.2 percent of the global population, or about 673 million people, experienced hunger in 2024,"** down from 8.5% in 2023 and 8.7% in 2022. The report gives a range of **"between 638 and 720 million people"** for 2024.
- **"around 2.3 billion people" faced moderate or severe food insecurity in 2024** — consistent with the figure already noted in [un-sdg2-targets.md](un-sdg2-targets.md).
- Regional split is uneven: **"The proportion of the population facing hunger in Africa surpassed 20 percent in 2024, affecting 307 million people,"** while **"an estimated 12.7 percent of the population, or more than 39 million people, may have faced hunger in 2024"** in Western Asia. By contrast, Asia's undernourishment fell "from 7.9 percent in 2022 to 6.7 percent."
- Healthy-diet affordability, a theme new to this report versus the other UN source already in this repo: in low-income countries, **"the number of people unable to afford a healthy diet increased from 464 million in 2019 to 545 million in 2024."**
- Projection: **"512 million people could be chronically undernourished by 2030. Almost 60 percent of those will be in Africa."**

## ⚠️ How this relates to the other UN source already here

This report's 8.2% undernourishment and 2.3 billion food-insecure figures match [un-sdg2-targets.md](un-sdg2-targets.md) exactly — same underlying UN data, different framing. The new information here is the regional breakdown (Africa, Western Asia) and the healthy-diet-affordability angle (545 million in low-income countries), which the SDG2 targets source doesn't cover. As with the other global figures already in this wiki, don't average or directly equate this global data with the LA County, U.S. national, or Seoul figures — different populations and instruments.

## Summary

The UN's 2025 SOFI report shows global hunger easing slightly for a second straight year (8.2% in 2024, down from 8.7% in 2022), but the improvement is uneven: hunger keeps rising in Africa (now over 20%, 307 million people) and Western Asia, and the number of people in low-income countries unable to afford a healthy diet has grown from 464 million to 545 million since 2019 — meaning affordability, not just raw food availability, is a worsening global problem.
