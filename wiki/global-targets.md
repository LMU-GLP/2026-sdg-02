# Global and national context: SDG Target 2.1 and U.S. data

## The UN target this challenge maps to

**Target 2.1:** "By 2030, end hunger and ensure access by all people, in particular the poor and people in vulnerable situations, including infants, to safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year round" — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

It's measured by two indicators: prevalence of undernourishment, and prevalence of moderate or severe food insecurity based on the Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES) — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

Target 2.2 separately calls out "the nutritional needs of ... older persons," which is directly relevant to the Seoul evidence in this wiki — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

## Global scale

- **8.2% of the global population were undernourished in 2024**, down from 8.7% in 2022 — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md). The 2025 SOFI report gives the same rate in absolute terms: **"8.2 percent of the global population, or about 673 million people, experienced hunger in 2024,"** down from 8.5% in 2023 and 8.7% in 2022, with a stated range of "between 638 and 720 million people" — [UN SOFI 2025 report](../sources/un-sofi-global-hunger-report.md).
- **"around 2.3 billion people were moderately or severely food insecure" in 2024** — confirmed in both [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md) and the [UN SOFI 2025 report](../sources/un-sofi-global-hunger-report.md).
- Global childhood stunting fell from 26.4% (2012) to 23.2% (2024); 30.7% of women of reproductive age were affected by anaemia in 2023 — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

## The uneven regional picture (new: SOFI 2025)

The global average hides sharp regional gaps:

- **Africa: "The proportion of the population facing hunger in Africa surpassed 20 percent in 2024, affecting 307 million people."**
- **Western Asia: "an estimated 12.7 percent of the population, or more than 39 million people, may have faced hunger in 2024."**
- By contrast, Asia's undernourishment improved, falling "from 7.9 percent in 2022 to 6.7 percent."
- Looking ahead, the report projects **"512 million people could be chronically undernourished by 2030. Almost 60 percent of those will be in Africa."**

All figures — [UN SOFI 2025 report](../sources/un-sofi-global-hunger-report.md).

## Affordability, not just availability (new: SOFI 2025)

The SOFI 2025 report's central theme is that rising food prices are pricing people out of healthy diets even where raw hunger has eased: in low-income countries, **"the number of people unable to afford a healthy diet increased from 464 million in 2019 to 545 million in 2024"** — [UN SOFI 2025 report](../sources/un-sofi-global-hunger-report.md). This is the same affordability dynamic the LA evidence describes at a county level (see [Los Angeles](los-angeles.md)), just at global scale and among low-income countries rather than a high-cost-of-living U.S. county — the two are not the same population or measurement and shouldn't be equated, but they point the same direction.

## U.S. national scale

- **13.7% (18.3 million) of U.S. households were food insecure at some time during 2024**, of which 5.4% (7.2 million) had the more severe "very low food security" — [USDA food security dataset](../sources/usda-food-security-dataset.md).
- This is measured with the same USDA Household Food Security Module used in the LA County brief, so it's directly comparable: LA County's 24% runs nearly double the 13.7% national rate — [USDA food security dataset](../sources/usda-food-security-dataset.md), [la-food-insecurity.md](../sources/la-food-insecurity.md).

## ⚠️ Don't average the global, national, and local figures together

Global (8.2% undernourished / 673 million people / 2.3 billion food insecure), U.S. national (13.7% household food insecurity), LA County (24%), and Seoul elderly (56.9%) all come from different instruments and different populations. Only the U.S. national and LA County figures share the same instrument (USDA module) and are safely comparable to each other. See [LA vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md) for why the Seoul figure can't be compared to LA's.
