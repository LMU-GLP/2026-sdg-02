# Global and national context: SDG Target 2.1 and U.S. data

## The UN target this challenge maps to

**Target 2.1:** "By 2030, end hunger and ensure access by all people, in particular the poor and people in vulnerable situations, including infants, to safe, nutritious and sufficient food all year round" — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

It's measured by two indicators: prevalence of undernourishment, and prevalence of moderate or severe food insecurity based on the Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES) — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

Target 2.2 separately calls out "the nutritional needs of ... older persons," which is directly relevant to the Seoul evidence in this wiki — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

## Global scale

- **8.2% of the global population were undernourished in 2024**, down from 8.7% in 2022 — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).
- **"around 2.3 billion people were moderately or severely food insecure" in 2024** — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).
- Global childhood stunting fell from 26.4% (2012) to 23.2% (2024); 30.7% of women of reproductive age were affected by anaemia in 2023 — [UN SDG2 targets](../sources/un-sdg2-targets.md).

## U.S. national scale

- **13.7% (18.3 million) of U.S. households were food insecure at some time during 2024**, of which 5.4% (7.2 million) had the more severe "very low food security" — [USDA food security dataset](../sources/usda-food-security-dataset.md).
- This is measured with the same USDA Household Food Security Module used in the LA County brief, so it's directly comparable: LA County's 24% runs nearly double the 13.7% national rate — [USDA food security dataset](../sources/usda-food-security-dataset.md), [la-food-insecurity.md](../sources/la-food-insecurity.md).

## ⚠️ Don't average the global, national, and local figures together

Global (8.2% undernourished / 2.3 billion food insecure), U.S. national (13.7% household food insecurity), LA County (24%), and Seoul elderly (56.9%) all come from different instruments and different populations. Only the U.S. national and LA County figures share the same instrument (USDA module) and are safely comparable to each other. See [LA vs. South Korea](la-vs-south-korea.md) for why the Seoul figure can't be compared to LA's.
