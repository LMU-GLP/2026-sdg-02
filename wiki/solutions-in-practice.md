# Solutions already in practice

## Everytable (Los Angeles) — income-scaled pricing

[Everytable](../sources/everytable-program.md) is a public benefit corporation, founded 2016 in LA County, that sells fresh meals with **"pricing scaled to the income level of the neighborhoods it serves,"** using per-zip-code income data — [everytable-program.md](../sources/everytable-program.md).

- Now operates **more than 35 locations across California**.
- During COVID-19, scaled weekly production from 30,000 to 180,000 meals and sold **5.2 million meals total**, growing annual revenue from $6 million to $36 million.
- Raised **$87 million** in total funding since founding.
- Stated goal: provide "affordable and healthy meals to the local community while remaining competitive against alternatives such as fast food restaurants."

All figures — [everytable-program.md](../sources/everytable-program.md).

This is direct evidence that a business model addressing LA's specific affordability gap (see [Los Angeles](los-angeles.md)) already operates at meaningful scale. No source currently in this repo says what share of LA's 24% food-insecure households Everytable actually reaches.

## Seoul — a recommendation, not (yet) a program

The Seoul elderly study doesn't point to an existing program, but does make a specific, evidence-backed recommendation of its own:

> "Educational programs designed to enhance the ability to purchase online food, use self-service kiosks in restaurants, and use food delivery apps would effectively decrease food insecurity among the elderly in Seoul." — [korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md](../sources/korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md)

This is grounded in the study's own finding that higher digital food literacy was protective against food insecurity (OR 0.63) — [korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md](../sources/korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md). No source here documents such a program actually running in Seoul; this is a gap, not a placeholder to fill in without a source.
