# Concept: a water-soluble nutrient pod for low-food-access areas

This is the team's own proposed solution, built by answering questions rather
than assumed — see the interview below the concept for how each choice was
made.

## Who it's for

Residents of Fresno County neighborhoods with a low Food Environment Index
score — places that are low-income and far from a grocery store, the same
low-food-access population the [Food Environment Index](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md)
already measures and the [app concept](app-concept.md) maps — [Fresno County: the data](fresno-county.md).

## What it is

A solid, water-soluble pod — dropped into a cup of plain water, where it
dissolves and releases nutrients into the water. It is not a pre-mixed bottled
drink and not an edible/chewable tablet; the water itself is supplied by the
person drinking it.

## What it delivers

The pod is built around the six basic nutrient categories: **carbohydrate,
protein, fat, water, minerals, and vitamins** — as chosen by the team, not
targeted at a specific documented deficiency figure from any source in this
repo. If a claim like "closes X% of a specific vitamin gap" is needed later,
that number has to come from a nutrition source, not be assumed.

## How it would actually reach people

Two roles, kept separate:

- **Funding and manufacturing**: large supermarket chains and food
  manufacturers, the same role The Wonderful Company already plays in Fresno
  County — its $2.5 million donation built Central California Food Bank's
  "Wonderful Food Center" — [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md).
- **Last-mile distribution**: Central California Food Bank's existing routes
  into low-access areas — Mobile Pantry, School Pantry, and the BackPack
  program — rather than retail sale, since the target population is defined
  by being far from the retail channel a supermarket partner would normally
  use — [Programs already working on this in Fresno](programs-in-fresno.md).

This mirrors a funding relationship that already exists in Fresno County
(corporate money, food-bank distribution), rather than proposing a new kind of
partnership from scratch.

## What this deliberately does not solve

This is explicitly a supplement, not a replacement, for fresh produce access.
It does not claim to raise Fresno County's Food Environment Index score or
close the gap documented in [Fresno County: the data](fresno-county.md) — a
pod of nutrients is not the same thing as a grocery store carrying fresh food
within reach, and this concept doesn't pretend otherwise.

## Open questions, not yet answered

- Flavor and kid-friendliness of a first version
- Whether it's free to the recipient (donated through the food bank, as the
  Wonderful Company funding model would suggest) or sold at any price
- Shelf life, safety/regulatory review, and manufacturing cost — none of
  which have a source in this repo yet
