# Programs already working on this in Fresno

Two different organizations are already active in Fresno County, and they take two
different approaches to the same problem: one distributes food directly, the other
changes the food environment itself.

## Central California Food Bank — direct distribution

[Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md) serves
Fresno, Madera, Tulare, Kings, and Kern Counties, and **"provides food to more than
280,000 people each month, including nearly 100,000 children"** — [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md).

Its programs include:

- **Neighborhood Market and Mobile Pantry** — fresh produce distribution in low-income and rural areas.
- **BackPack program** — **"supplies 1,300 elementary school children a backpack of food every Friday"** to take home over the weekend.
- **School Pantry program** and the **USDA Emergency Food Assistance Program**.
- **CalFresh Outreach** — helps low-income families apply for SNAP benefits.
- A **Partner Pantry Program** working with **"more than 220 partner food pantries."**

All figures — [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md).

It has already attracted large-scale corporate funding from local agribusiness: **The
Wonderful Company gave a $2.5 million donation** that built **"The Wonderful Food
Center"** (opened 2018), which **"triples the capacity of the food bank"** — [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md). That's direct evidence a Central Valley company will fund food-access infrastructure at million-dollar scale.

## Cultiva La Salud — changing the food environment

[Cultiva La Salud](../sources/cultiva-la-salud.md) is headquartered in Fresno and
works across eight Central Valley counties, including Fresno. Rather than
distributing food itself, it changes what's already there:

- **"converting existing food vendors in the city's food deserts into healthy food
  vendors for the Latino community — including outfitting vendors with new bicycle
  carts"** — a program that won the state's **"Let's Get Healthy California
  Innovation Challenge"** — [Cultiva La Salud](../sources/cultiva-la-salud.md).
- **"provides medically tailored food boxes to elder Latinas, age 65 and older
  living in Fresno County"** — [Cultiva La Salud](../sources/cultiva-la-salud.md).
- Farmers markets, community gardens, and school farm stands "in underserved
  neighborhoods," plus a "HOPE Pathways Community HUB" for health navigation —
  [Cultiva La Salud](../sources/cultiva-la-salud.md).

No source read gives a number of vendors converted, people served, or dollars
raised for Cultiva La Salud's work — that's a real gap, not a placeholder to fill
in without a source — [Cultiva La Salud](../sources/cultiva-la-salud.md).

## Why both matter for the app concept

Central California Food Bank already has the distribution network and CalFresh
enrollment pipeline; Cultiva La Salud already has the neighborhood-level food
environment relationships (converted vendors, farmers markets, community gardens).
An app that maps where Fresno County's need is worst has an immediate answer for
"then what" for both kinds of location, because both kinds of resource already
exist in the county — see [App concept](app-concept.md).
