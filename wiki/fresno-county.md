# Fresno County: the data

Fresno County shows up worse than both California and the U.S. on every food-access
indicator read so far, and its adult overweight/obesity rate runs well above both
benchmarks too — the two problems appear to be sitting on top of each other in the
same county, which is the reason this wiki proposes [an app](app-concept.md) that
maps where they overlap.

## Food access

- **Food Insecurity Rate: 17% (2023)** in Fresno County, versus 13.7% for California and 14.5% for the U.S. — "in the second worst 25% of U.S. Counties" — [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md).
- **Child Food Insecurity Rate: 21.8% (2023)**, versus 16.9% for California and 18.4% for the U.S. — [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md).
- **Food Environment Index: 7.5 out of 10** (10 = best), versus 8.7 for California and 7.6 for the U.S. This index "combines two measures of food access: the percentage of the population that is low-income and has low access to a grocery store, and the percentage of the population that did not have access to a reliable source of food during the past year (food insecurity)" — [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md).
- **60.8% of students are eligible for the Free Lunch Program** (as of May 1, 2026), a rough proxy for how many Fresno County households are living close to the poverty line — [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md).

## ⚠️ A second, disagreeing figure exists: 22%

Central California Food Bank's own materials state that **"the Fresno region has the third worst food hardship rate in the nation at 22%,"** with no date attached to that number — [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md). That does not match the 17% (2023) figure above. They may use different geographic boundaries ("Fresno region" vs. Fresno County) or a different year of data; nothing read so far reconciles them. Both are kept here rather than averaged or resolved by picking one — see [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md) for the full caveat.

## Diet and weight

**70.1% of adults in Fresno County are overweight or obese (2024, California Health Interview Survey)**, versus 60.9% for California and 68.5% for the U.S. — "in the worst 25% of CA Counties" — [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md).

## Why this pairing matters

Fresno County isn't just food-insecure; it's food-insecure *and* higher-obesity at the same time, both worse than the state and national baselines. A low Food Environment Index score and a high overweight/obesity rate are consistent with the same underlying condition — many Fresno County residents live somewhere without regular access to a grocery store carrying affordable, healthy food — but no source read so far maps *where inside the county* both problems are worst at once. That gap is exactly what [the proposed app](app-concept.md) is built to close.
