# Los Angeles vs. South Korea (Seoul): comparing the evidence

This team hasn't picked a region yet. This page lays the two candidate regions
side by side using only what's in `sources/`.

## The headline figures, side by side

| | Los Angeles County | Seoul (elderly 65+) |
|---|---|---|
| Headline rate | **24%** of all households food insecure (Oct 2025) | **56.9%** of 513 seniors surveyed food-insecure |
| Who was measured | All county households | Only residents aged 65+ |
| Instrument | USDA Household Food Security Module | Single-item variety-based question, Seoul Food Survey 2023 |
| Primary driver named | Cost of living ("about 50% more than the national average") plus 2025 wildfires and immigration-enforcement fear | Environmental (38.0%) and physical (37.5%) barriers outweigh financial (24.5%) |
| Source | [la-food-insecurity.md](../sources/la-food-insecurity.md) | [korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md](../sources/korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md) |

## ⚠️ These two headline numbers do not measure the same thing

The Korea source itself flags this directly:

> "This study counts anyone who lacks *variety* as food insecure ('enough but not always various kinds of food' = 37.3 of the 56.9 points). The USC L.A. brief uses the USDA module, which is about not having enough food at all. Comparing the two headline numbers would be wrong." — [korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md](../sources/korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md)

The closer like-for-like figure, per that same source, is the **19.6%** of surveyed Seoul seniors who "sometimes" or "often" did not have enough to eat — closer in kind to LA's 24%, though the populations (all households vs. seniors only) and instruments still differ, so even this isn't an exact comparison — [korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md](../sources/korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md).

## What each region's evidence is actually about

- **LA's evidence is broad**: all households, a five-year trend (17% → 30% → 24%), and a named cause tied to cost of living and 2025 shocks (wildfires, immigration enforcement fear) — [la-food-insecurity.md](../sources/la-food-insecurity.md).
- **Seoul's evidence is narrow but deep**: one age group (65+), with a specific causal breakdown (environmental > physical > financial) and two measured protective factors (digital food literacy, food environment satisfaction) — [korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md](../sources/korea-elderly-food-insecurity.md).
- Only LA has a currently-sourced solution already operating: [Everytable](../sources/everytable-program.md), see [Solutions in practice](solutions-in-practice.md). No source here documents an existing program in Seoul — only the study's own recommendation for one.
