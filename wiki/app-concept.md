# App concept: mapping obesity and food-access gaps in Fresno County

## The problem this app targets

Fresno County scores worse than California and the U.S. on food access — a Food
Environment Index of 7.5/10 versus 8.7 (CA) and 7.6 (U.S.), and a 17% food
insecurity rate versus 13.7% (CA) and 14.5% (U.S.) — while its adult
overweight-or-obesity rate (70.1%) also runs above both benchmarks (60.9% CA,
68.5% U.S.) — [Fresno County: the data](fresno-county.md),
[Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md).

Both problems are known, county-wide. What's missing from every source read for
this wiki is a *map* — nothing here says which specific neighborhoods, ZIP codes,
or census tracts inside Fresno County have both problems worst at the same time.
That's the gap this app concept fills.

## What the app would do

1. **Overlay two layers on one map of Fresno County:**
   - A layer of **overweight/obesity prevalence** by neighborhood or ZIP code.
   - A layer of **low-food-access areas** — places that are low-income and far
     from a grocery store, the same definition used in the Food Environment Index
     — [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md).
2. **Highlight the overlap** — areas that are simultaneously high-obesity *and*
   low-food-access — as priority zones. The county-wide numbers above show this
   overlap exists somewhere in Fresno County; the app is what would show exactly
   where.
3. **Connect each priority zone to what already exists there.** For each
   highlighted area, surface the nearest real resources documented in this wiki:
   - Central California Food Bank's Neighborhood Market, Mobile Pantry, School
     Pantry, or BackPack program sites, or its CalFresh Outreach enrollment help
     — [Programs in Fresno](programs-in-fresno.md).
   - Cultiva La Salud's converted healthy food vendors, farmers markets, community
     gardens, or its medically tailored food boxes for seniors — [Programs in Fresno](programs-in-fresno.md).
4. **Let a family search their own address** and see, in plain language, whether
   they're in a priority zone and what's nearest to them.

## What this app is not, yet

This is a concept, not a built product, and it should be pitched as one. No
source gathered for this wiki gives **tract-level or ZIP-level** obesity or
food-access data for Fresno County — every figure above is a **county-wide**
average. Building the actual map would require sourcing that finer-grained data
first (for example, the CDC's PLACES project publishes census-tract obesity
estimates, and the USDA Food Access Research Atlas maps low-income/low-access
tracts nationally) — neither of those is a source in this repo yet, and no
figure from either should be used until it is.

## Finding and pitching sponsors

The app needs a funder to build it and an operator to keep the data current
after launch. Three types of sponsor are realistic for Fresno County
specifically, because each one is already active in this exact space:

### Companies

The Wonderful Company already funds food-access infrastructure in Fresno County
at seven-figure scale: its **$2.5 million donation** built Central California
Food Bank's **"Wonderful Food Center,"** which **"triples the capacity of the
food bank"** — [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md).
Central Valley agribusiness is the natural first call for corporate sponsorship,
because the pitch is close to home for them: their own workforce and neighboring
communities are the population the map would serve.

### Local government

Fresno County Public Health already runs the data platform this wiki's Fresno
figures come from — [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md)
— which means the county already collects and publishes the kind of data this
app would visualize. That makes county Public Health a plausible co-sponsor or
data partner, not just a target audience, since a public-facing map complements
data it already tracks internally.

### Foundations

Cultiva La Salud's food-vendor-conversion program already won the state's
**"Let's Get Healthy California Innovation Challenge"** — [Cultiva La Salud](../sources/cultiva-la-salud.md)
— which shows that state-level innovation grants specifically for Fresno-area
food-access projects already exist and have already funded a comparable, smaller
idea. That's the model to point to when approaching health-focused foundations
or the same state innovation-challenge program.

### How to structure the pitch

1. **Open with the pairing, not just one number**: Fresno County is worse than
   the state and national average on food access *and* on obesity, at the same
   time — [Fresno County: the data](fresno-county.md).
2. **Name the gap**: no existing source maps where both problems overlap inside
   the county — that's the specific thing new funding buys.
3. **Point at who already funds this kind of work here**: The Wonderful
   Company's $2.5 million gift, the county's own public health data platform,
   and Cultiva La Salud's state innovation-challenge win are three separate,
   already-real precedents in Fresno County for a sponsor of this exact type —
   [Central California Food Bank](../sources/central-california-food-bank.md),
   [Healthy Fresno County Data](../sources/fresno-county-food-access-data.md),
   [Cultiva La Salud](../sources/cultiva-la-salud.md).
4. **Ask for what sustains it, not just what builds it**: tract-level data
   licensing or a data-sharing agreement (likely the ongoing cost) alongside a
   one-time build grant.

## Related concept

This app is a discovery and routing tool — it finds priority areas and points
people at existing resources, it doesn't provide food or nutrients itself. The
team's other proposed solution, [a water-soluble nutrient pod](nutrient-pod-concept.md),
is a product for the same low-food-access population; the two are
complementary, not competing, and nothing here assumes the team has to pick
only one.
